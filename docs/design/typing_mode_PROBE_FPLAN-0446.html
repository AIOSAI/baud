TYPING MODE — shape probe

FPLAN-0446 round 4. Three doors that give the keyboard back to every terminal room — a bash prompt, a REPL, a TUI, Claude Code. Tap the door in each mock: the keyboard comes up, the room slides so the line you are typing stays visible, and the room hears each character as it lands. Same engine behind all three; only the door and the readout differ.

Switch the room below — the door is the same door either way. That is the point.

A · THE KBD CHIP

A twelfth chip on the row that already owns every other byte-writing control. Tap to arm, tap to stop.

Wins: the door is in the same place every time, so it needs no aim — the mis-aim trap cannot come back through it. It is the row that already owns the one-pixel field, so it is the smallest change to the shipped shape. Costs zero vertical space.

Costs, now measured rather than guessed: as a twelfth flexing chip this door came out 21×25px — under half Android's 48dp minimum, and Patrick could not hit it on the device. It is now pinned to a fixed 44px, which it takes out of the eleven chips beside it. That is door A's real price: the whole chip row gets tighter to make one door reliable. The baud composer is also hidden while armed, so returning to baud is two taps, not one.

B · THE LANE SWITCH

The compose bar keeps its shape and gains a two-state pill at its left edge: baud or room. In cc the box stops holding text and becomes a pass-through — what you type is already at the room's prompt.

Wins: the door is where the thumb already goes to type, and one tap switches back. The two destinations are visibly one surface with a switch, which is the honest mental model — there is only ever one place your keys are going.

Costs: a box that looks like it holds text and does not is the exact confusion this round exists to kill. It needs the amber and the changed placeholder to carry the whole warning, and it eats width the paperclip and send buttons are already competing for.

C · THE TYPE BAR

A permanent full-width bar between the room and the belt. Idle it reads ⌨ type in the room; armed it becomes the status strip and says so in amber across the whole width.

Wins: unmissable in both states. The single biggest tap target of the three, and the one that can never be confused with the baud composer, because it is nowhere near it. Both composers stay on screen at once.

Costs: a permanent row of vertical space on a screen whose whole argument is rows — it is the only one of the three that takes rows away from the room even when you are not typing.

What is the same in all three: the room slides up when the keyboard opens rather than shrinking — the pty geometry never changes, so nothing in the room re-renders into a shorter pane and the frame-collision garble has nothing to collide. The top of the scrollback goes off screen; the box you are typing into does not. And because every character reaches the room as a real byte, everything interactive works again — a shell's tab-completion and history, a REPL, and Claude Code's slash menu filtering as you type. The deaf menu dies as a side effect rather than as a feature.
